Stock Screener Workflow: Data Check, Red Flags, Peer Gate, Risk / Reward, Decision
=================================================================================

Purpose
-------

This workflow is meant to help rank research ideas. It is not a buy signal.

The goal is to avoid pretending one early score can tell whether a company is
good. Most financial numbers only become meaningful after comparing the company
with similar companies.

Simple idea:

    Data tells us whether we can trust the numbers.
    Red flags tell us whether something is obviously dangerous.
    Peers tell us whether the company is better or worse than alternatives.
    Risk / Reward tells us whether the possible upside is worth the downside.
    Decision / Action tells us whether to leave the app and do real research.


Final App Workflow
------------------

0. Idea Entry
    Pick a ticker from Sector Funnel or Flag Review.

1. Data Check
    Can we trust the app's data enough to continue?

2. Red Flag Check
    Is anything obviously dangerous before peer comparison?

3. Peer Gate
    Is this company actually better than similar companies?

4. Risk / Reward vs ETF
    Is the upside worth the downside and better than the ETF alternative?

5. Decision / Action
    Should I leave the screener and do real company research?


1. Data Check
-------------

Main question:

    Can we trust the app's data enough to continue?

This happens before peer comparison.

What belongs here:

    Data Score
        Can the app trust the local data?

    Missing data
        Are key fields missing, such as revenue, cash, debt, shares, price, or
        free cash flow?

    Source quality
        Did the data come from usable local sources?

    Peer group assigned
        Does the ticker have a peer group so the app can compare it properly?

What this step should NOT decide:

    It should not decide whether margins, valuation, balance sheet, or dilution
    are good. Those need peer context.

Possible result:

    Continue
    Needs Proof
    Pass For Now


2. Red Flag Check
-----------------

Main question:

    Is anything obviously dangerous before we even compare peers?

This is not trying to find "good" companies. It only catches obvious danger.

Red flags to check:

    Negative free cash flow with short cash runway
        The company may need to raise money soon.

    Extreme dilution
        Shareholders may be getting watered down badly.

    Dangerous liquidity
        Cash, debt, or current ratio may point to financial stress.

    Missing core data
        The app cannot trust the review.

    No peer group
        The app cannot judge quality or valuation correctly.

Possible result:

    Continue
    Needs Proof
    Pass For Now


3. Peer Gate
------------

Main question:

    Is this company actually better than similar companies?

This is where company numbers become meaningful. A company does not have one
universal "healthy" number for margins, valuation, balance sheet, dilution, or
free cash flow. Those numbers need to be compared with similar companies.

Peer Gate should compare:

    Quality
        Are margins, profitability, and cash conversion better or worse than
        the peer group?

    Valuation
        Is the market paying less, similar, or more for this company compared
        with peers using EV/Revenue, EV/FCF, P/S, P/E, and other available
        multiples?

    Balance sheet
        Does the company have a stronger or weaker financial cushion than
        similar companies?

    Dilution
        Is the company creating fewer, similar, or more new shares than peers?

    Free cash flow
        Is free-cash-flow strength better, worse, or average compared with
        similar companies?

Peer Gate should answer:

    Is this stock better, worse, or average compared with alternatives?


4. Risk / Reward Tab
--------------------

Main question:

    If this company does worse, normal, or better than expected, what could the
    stock be worth?

This does not predict the future perfectly. It creates a rough value range.


4A. Build Scenarios From History
--------------------------------

The app looks at historical company data.

Bear case:
    The company underperforms.

    Uses:
        Weak historical growth
        Weak historical margins
        Stress haircut if recent history had no bad years

Base case:
    The company stays on track.

    Uses:
        Normal or median historical growth
        Normal or median historical margins

Bull case:
    The company overperforms.

    Uses:
        Strong historical growth
        Strong historical margins

Example:

    Historical revenue growth:
        Year 1: 12%
        Year 2: 18%
        Year 3: 22%

    Scenario assumptions:
        Bear: 6% growth
        Base: 17% growth
        Bull: 22% growth

Important rule:

    If the company had no bad years recently, the bear case should not pretend
    risk does not exist. The app should apply a stress haircut.


4B. Use Peer Multiples
----------------------

Peers help estimate what the market might pay for the company's future
performance.

Simple rule:

    Bear multiple:
        Below peer median

    Base multiple:
        Near peer median

    Bull multiple:
        Above peer median

History tells us how the business might perform.
Peers tell us what that performance might be worth.


4C. Run Four Valuation Models
-----------------------------

For each scenario, the app estimates value four ways.

1. EV/Revenue

    Plain English:
        What is the company worth based on sales?

    Simple formula:
        Future Revenue = Current Revenue * (1 + growth rate) ^ years
        Future EV = Future Revenue * EV/Revenue multiple
        Equity Value = Future EV + Cash - Debt
        Estimated Price = Equity Value / Future Shares

2. EV/FCF

    Plain English:
        What is the company worth based on cash it keeps?

    Simple formula:
        Future FCF = Future Revenue * FCF margin
        Future EV = Future FCF * EV/FCF multiple
        Equity Value = Future EV + Cash - Debt
        Estimated Price = Equity Value / Future Shares

3. P/E

    Plain English:
        What is the company worth based on earnings?

    Simple formula:
        Future Earnings = Future Revenue * net margin
        Equity Value = Future Earnings * P/E multiple
        Estimated Price = Equity Value / Future Shares

4. DCF

    Plain English:
        What is the company worth based on future cash flows?

    Simple formula:
        Estimate future free cash flow for several years.
        Discount those cash flows back to today.
        Add terminal value.
        Add cash and subtract debt.
        Divide by future shares.

Important rule:

    The app should not simple-average all four methods blindly.
    Each method should get a reliability weight.

Example reliability:

    EV/Revenue:
        More useful for growth companies or companies with weak profits.

    EV/FCF:
        More useful when free cash flow is positive and stable.

    P/E:
        More useful when earnings are positive and meaningful.

    DCF:
        More useful when cash flows are stable enough to forecast.


4D. Blend The Values
--------------------

Each scenario gets values from the four models.

Example:

    Scenario    EV/Revenue    EV/FCF    P/E     DCF     Blended Value
    Bear        $70           $65       $72     $68     $69
    Base        $115          $122      $118    $110    $116
    Bull        $160          $172      $165    $150    $162

The blended value should be a weighted average.

Weak or unreliable methods count less.
Skipped methods count zero.


4E. Compare To Current Price
----------------------------

Example:

    Current price: $100

    Bear value: $69
    Bear downside: -31%

    Base value: $116
    Base upside: +16%

    Bull value: $162
    Bull upside: +62%

Formula:

    Upside or downside = Estimated Value / Current Price - 1


4F. Risk / Reward Conclusion
----------------------------

The app should give a plain-English conclusion.

Possible labels:

    Weak
        Base upside is small and bear downside is large.

    Okay
        Base upside is decent, but downside is meaningful.

    Strong
        Base upside is attractive and bear downside is survivable.

    Speculative
        Only the bull case works.

Final Risk / Reward output:

    Bear downside
    Base upside
    Bull upside
    Expected value
    Risk/reward label
    Key assumption


4G. Compare Against The ETF Alternative
---------------------------------------

Main question:

    Is the single stock worth owning instead of the easier ETF alternative?

This is important because a stock can look attractive by itself but still not
be worth the extra single-company risk.

The app should compare:

    Stock bear / base / bull
    vs
    ETF bear / base / bull

Example:

    Scenario    Stock Value    Stock Return    ETF Return    Stock Advantage
    Bear        $69            -31%            -18%          -13% worse
    Base        $116           +16%            +12%          +4% better
    Bull        $162           +62%            +30%          +32% better

Plain-English read:

    If the stock only gives a little more base-case upside than the ETF, but has
    much worse bear-case downside, it may not be worth the risk.

    If the stock gives much better base-case upside than the ETF and the bear
    case is survivable, it may deserve a deeper look.

How the app can estimate ETF scenarios:

    Bear ETF case:
        Use recent bad-period return, max drawdown, or downside volatility.

    Base ETF case:
        Use normal historical return over the selected lookback period.

    Bull ETF case:
        Use strong-period return or upside momentum over the selected lookback
        period.

Decision rule:

    The stock should usually need at least one of these:

        Clearly better base-case upside than the ETF
        Similar upside but better downside risk
        Much better bull case with survivable bear case
        A specific catalyst the ETF does not capture

If none of those are true:

    The ETF may be the better choice.


5. Decision / Action
--------------------

Main question:

    Should I leave the screener and do real company research?

This is not another math step. It is the handoff point.

The app should summarize:

    Data Check
        Clean enough / needs proof / pass

    Red Flag Check
        No obvious danger / needs proof / pass

    Peer Gate
        Strong vs peers / mixed / weak

    Risk / Reward vs ETF
        Strong / okay / weak / speculative

    Decision
        Pass / Watchlist / Deep Dive / Candidate Position

Decision buckets:

    Pass
        Not worth more time right now.

    Watchlist
        Interesting, but needs a better price, cleaner data, or a catalyst.

    Deep Dive
        The ticker earned serious company research outside the screener.

    Candidate Position
        Only after deeper research, thesis, valuation, risk, ETF alternative,
        and kill criteria are clear.

Example:

    Data Check: Continue
    Red Flag Check: Continue
    Peer Gate: Strong vs peers
    Risk / Reward vs ETF: Strong
    Decision: Deep Dive
    Reason: Worth leaving the screener for company research.
    Next action: Read 10-K business section and risk factors.

Another example:

    Data Check: Continue
    Red Flag Check: Continue
    Peer Gate: Mixed
    Risk / Reward vs ETF: Weak
    Decision: Pass
    Reason: ETF gives similar upside with less single-company risk.
    Next action: Use ETF or review another ticker.


After The Screener
------------------

If the decision is Deep Dive, the user leaves the screener and starts company
research.

Company research belongs in a separate memo/research phase:

    Understand the business.
    Read filings.
    Build or refine valuation assumptions.
    Identify the variant view.
    Write the pre-mortem.
    Decide Pass, Watchlist, or Candidate Position.

Portfolio sizing belongs after a ticker becomes a true Candidate Position.


Simple Summary
--------------

Idea Entry:
    Pick the ticker to review from Sector Funnel or Flag Review.

Data Check:
    Can we trust the numbers?

Red Flag Check:
    Is anything obviously dangerous?

Peer Gate:
    Is this company better than similar companies?

Risk / Reward:
    If the company does worse, normal, or better, is the possible upside worth
    the possible downside?

ETF Alternative:
    Is the stock worth the extra single-company risk compared with the easier
    ETF choice?

Decision / Action:
    Is this worth leaving the screener for deeper company research?

Short version:

    History says how the business might perform.
    Peers say what that performance might be worth.
    Current price tells us whether the upside is worth the downside.
    The ETF comparison tells us whether the stock is worth owning instead of
    the basket.
    The decision step tells us whether to pass, watch, deep dive, or consider a
    candidate position after real research.
